## Summary

**Why the 3DS strands games, and it is not mainly the hardware.** The obvious
answer is the two screens, the stereoscopic 3D and StreetPass, and those do
block some titles: `Kid Icarus: Uprising` needs a stylus-and-shoulder
control scheme with no modern equivalent, and everything built on StreetPass died
with the local wireless network.

But the real cause is commercial, and it is the exact **inverse of the Wii U**.
The Wii U sold 13.56M, so almost nobody played its library and Nintendo has spent
a decade profitably re-selling it. The 3DS sold **75.94M**, nearly six times as
many, so its audience already owns those games. There is far less money in
re-selling `Mario Kart 7` to people who bought it than in re-selling `Mario Kart 8`
to people who never had a Wii U. That is why 28 of 69 Wii U debut-exclusives have
escaped, against only 14 of 136 here.

The exceptions prove it: the 3DS games that did get ported are mostly ones whose
3DS release **underperformed** (`Luigi's Mansion: Dark Moon`, `Miitopia`) or which
belong to a series with ongoing commercial momentum (`Monster Hunter Stories`,
`Rune Factory 4`).

## Contested

Under the debut rule most disputes resolve themselves. One remains.

| Title | Year | Verdict | Case for including | Case against |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Super Smash Bros. for Nintendo 3DS | 2014 | **Include** | A separate build from the Wii U version (different stages, different modes (Smash Run), different engine target) and it shipped **first** (Sep 2014 JP, vs Nov 2014). If either half of the pair is a debut, this is the one. | The two were marketed as one product line under a shared name and roster, so neither is cleanly "the game". Note the Wii U catalogue also includes its half; this is the one place both are counted. |

## Appendix: the Japanese licensed tail

Roughly 90 of the 238 category entries are Japan-only licensed tie-ins and small
retail titles: `Yo-kai Sangokushi`, `Inazuma Eleven GO` (three games), `LBX:
Little Battlers eXperience`, `My Hero Academia: Battle for All`, `Attack on Titan:
Humanity in Chains`, `The Disastrous Life of Saiki K.`, `Assassination Classroom`,
`Jewelpet`, `Tenkai Knights`, `Hero Bank`, `Super Robot Wars UX`, `Metal Max 4`,
`Langrisser Re:Incarnation Tensei`, `Puzzle & Dragons X`, `Hatsune Miku: Project
Mirai 1 & 2`, `GameCenter CX`, `Slime Mori Mori Dragon Quest 3`, `Theatrhythm
Dragon Quest`, `Dragon Quest Monsters: Joker 3`, `Puyo Puyo Chronicle`,
`Bakutsuri Bar Hunter`, the `Parascientific Escape` trilogy, and similar.

These share the PSP's stranding cause: **the licence, not the hardware**. They
are **not individually verified** and are excluded from the counts.

**Category noise warning.** Wikipedia's `Nintendo 3DS-only games` category
contains many entries that fail the debut test (`Ocarina of Time 3D`,
`Donkey Kong Country Returns 3D`, `Cave Story 3D`, `Super Mario Maker for
Nintendo 3DS`, the Untold remakes, `Bit.Trip Saga`). "3DS-only" and "3DS debut"
are different claims; filter before importing.

## Coverage gaps

- **Japanese tail.** ~90 category entries not individually verified.
- **eShop micro-releases.** The 3DS eShop's budget tail (Nintendo Web Framework-era
 shovelware, `Johnny Kung Fu`-style filler) is only partly covered.
- **StreetPass Mii Plaza games** are counted as one entry, not twelve.
- **Virtual Console** deliberately out of scope, re-releases by definition.
