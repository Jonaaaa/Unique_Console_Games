## ⚠ Sega's first console, released the same day as the Famicom

The SG-1000 launched on **15 July 1983 — the same day as Nintendo's Famicom** — and
lost decisively. It is the direct ancestor of the Master System, and Sega has never
reissued any of its library: not on Virtual Console, not on the Mega Drive Minis, not
in any Sega collection.

That leaves it with no reissues of any kind. Where an
SG-1000 game was later remade for the Master System, that Master System version is
usually what Sega reissues — the SG-1000 original stays behind.

**Why the SG-1000 strands games.** Sega's reissue programme begins at the Genesis and
reaches back only selectively to the Master System. The SG-1000 predates all of it,
sold two million units in four territories, and has no commercial constituency. There
is no rights obstacle — Sega owns nearly all of it — just no interest.

The platform's historical value is in **who made these games**: it is where Yuji Naka
and Yu Suzuki started.

## Appendix — the tail

- **Arcade conversions (~60 titles).** Sega converting its own 1981–83 cabinets.
 Most qualify as first home releases under the arcade rule, and essentially all are
 stranded — but individually minor.
- **SC-3000 computer software (~40 titles).** The SG-1000's keyboard variant shared
 cartridges; where a title was computer-first it is excluded.
- **Japan-only tail (~50 titles).** Mahjong, licensed anime and educational software.
- **Taiwan and Australia variants.** The console was sold in a few markets under other
 names (Aaronix, Grandstand), with no distinct library.

## Coverage gaps

- 10 of ~160 titles. English-language documentation of this library is the thinnest
 of any platform here.
- The arcade-conversion majority is characterised, not enumerated.
- No reissue programme exists, so these `Stranded` claims are among the most stable
 here.
