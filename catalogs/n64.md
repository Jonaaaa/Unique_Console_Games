## ⚠ Status caveat

Virtual Console, Nintendo Switch Online's N64 app, *Rare Replay*, and publisher
remasters all count as releases on another platform. Because the N64's canon is
dominated by Nintendo and Rare, both of whom actively re-sell their catalogues;
the `Ported` share is the **highest of any platform**.

**Why the N64 strands games.** The N64's canon escaped almost entirely: Nintendo
put its own catalogue on NSO, and Microsoft's 2002 purchase of Rare means
`Banjo-Kazooie`, `Perfect Dark`, `Conker`, `Blast Corps` and `Jet Force Gemini` are
all commercially available; Rare's N64 output is *more* accessible than most
platforms' first-party libraries.

What remains stranded splits three ways:

1. **Dead sports and wrestling licences.** The AKI wrestling games (`WWF No Mercy`,
 `WCW/nWo Revenge`) are considered the best wrestling games ever made and
 are locked behind WWE and the long-defunct WCW.
2. **The 64DD.** Japan's disk add-on sold ~15,000 units. Its games were distributed
 partly over the Randnet dial-up service, which is gone.
3. **Orphaned third parties.** `Castlevania 64`, `Body Harvest`, `Rocket: Robot on
 Wheels`: studios sold, folded, or moved on.

## Appendix: the tails

- **Sports annuals (~60 titles).** Madden, NBA Live, FIFA, NHL, WWF/WCW. Almost all
 permanently stranded on expired league and athlete licences, and almost all
 low-value except the AKI wrestling games.
- **64DD (9 retail titles).** The add-on sold ~15,000 units in Japan only. Every
 title is stranded, and the Randnet-dependent ones are unreproducible even on
 original hardware.
- **Japan-only retail (~40 titles).** Mostly licensed and mahjong titles.

## Coverage gaps

- Sports annuals characterised, not enumerated.
- NSO's N64 library rotates and grows; `Ported` is a floor.
