# Mattel Intellivision — Unique Games Catalogue

|---|---|

| **Lifespan** | 1979 – 1990; ~3M units sold |
| **Library size** | ~125 official titles |
| **Backward compat** | None. |
| **Last verified** | 2026-07-29 |
| **Coverage** | Near-complete for debut titles. | ## ⚠ The platform that had real sports licences in 1979

Mattel's differentiator was **officially licensed sports** — it secured MLB, NFL, NBA,
NHL and PGA rights at a time when Atari used generic names. That won it market share
against the 2600 and, four decades later, is exactly why that block of the library is
permanently stranded: those licences expired, and re-clearing five leagues plus player
associations for a 1980 cartridge is not a proposition anyone will fund.

It is an early example of licensing-as-strategy becoming licensing-as-tombstone — the same trap that later caught `Def Jam: Vendetta` and
`MLB The Show`.

| **Contested** | 1 | **Why the Intellivision strands games.** Two clean groups:

1. **The licensed sports catalogue** — MLB, NFL, NBA, NHL, PGA. Unreissuable.
2. **Third-party character licences** — `TRON`, `Advanced Dungeons & Dragons`,
 `Popeye`. Disney and Wizards of the Coast own those now.

Everything Mattel owned outright *has* been reissued, repeatedly: *Intellivision
Lives!* (2003), the Intellivision Flashback, and Evercade and Switch collections. So
the split here is clean — Mattel's own games survived, licensed ones did not.

- **Licensed sports (~15 titles).** The platform's commercial identity and its most
 reliably stranded block. Later collections include renamed versions with the league
 branding removed — which is a different product from what shipped.
- **Intellivoice titles (5).** `Space Spartans`, `B-17 Bomber`, `Bomb Squad`,
 `TRON: Solar Sailer` and `Tropical Trouble` required the speech module. Most were
 reissued; the module itself is a collector's item.
- **Arcade conversions (~30).** Mostly excluded on prior-home-release grounds, since
 Mattel, Atari and Coleco were all licensing the same cabinets simultaneously.
- **The Intellivision Amico (2020–).** An announced revival console that never
 properly shipped. No library exists, so it gets no file.

## Coverage gaps

- Complete for identifiable Mattel originals; the licensed conversions are excluded
 rather than uncatalogued.
- Third-party publishers (Imagic, Activision, Coleco-for-Intellivision) are only
 spot-covered.
- Mattel's reissue programme is active, so `Ported` may grow for titles it owns.
