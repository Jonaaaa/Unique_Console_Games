## ⚠ Atari's last console

The Jaguar was marketed as "64-bit" on the strength of two 32-bit coprocessors, was
hard to program, and sold roughly a quarter of a million units before
Atari merged with JTS in 1996 and exited hardware entirely. It has the **smallest
install base of any platform catalogued here**.

Its rights history is tangled: the Atari brand passed through JTS, Hasbro
Interactive, Infogrames and the current Atari SA, and in 1999 Hasbro **released the
Jaguar's technical specifications into the public domain** — which is why an active
homebrew scene still produces cartridges.

**Why the Jaguar strands games.** Three causes, and the third is unique to it.

1. **Film and franchise licences.** `Alien vs Predator` needs 20th Century Fox —
 the platform's best-regarded game, permanently locked.
2. **Defunct developers.** Rebellion survived; most others did not.
3. **Print runs measured in hundreds.** `Battlesphere` was produced in roughly a
 thousand copies by a hobbyist team and sells for four figures. Several late Jaguar
 releases exist in quantities smaller than some prototypes.

## Appendix — the tails

- **Jaguar CD (13 titles).** An add-on for a console with a quarter-million owners.
 Every CD title shipped into a market of a few thousand buyers, and almost none has
 been reissued. The narrowest release window of any hardware here.
- **Computer conversions (~15 titles).** Excluded on prior-home-release grounds.
- **Homebrew (~40 titles and counting).** Because the specifications are public
 domain, the Jaguar has an legitimate homebrew scene producing new
 commercial cartridges. Out of scope, but worth knowing it exists — it means the
 Jaguar's library is still growing thirty years after the console died.

## Coverage gaps

- Complete for notable official releases; minor licensed sports titles omitted.
- Homebrew and post-1996 independent releases are deliberately out of scope beyond
 `Battlesphere`, which was sold commercially and is widely catalogued.
