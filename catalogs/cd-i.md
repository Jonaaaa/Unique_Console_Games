## ⚠ Is this a console?

The CD-i was sold as a **home multimedia appliance**: encyclopaedias, music videos,
art collections, with games as one category among several. It attached to a TV, took
a controller and ran software from discs, so it is included here; but roughly
three-quarters of its catalogue is not games at all, and that portion is excluded.

Its place in history is an accident of licensing. In 1991 Philips secured the right to
use Nintendo characters, as fallout from the collapsed Nintendo–Philips CD-ROM
partnership. That produced **the only non-Nintendo-developed Zelda and Mario games
ever released**, and they are stranded permanently, because Nintendo will never
re-license them and Philips has no reason to.

**Why the CD-i strands games.** Three causes:

1. **The Nintendo licence.** Nintendo has never acknowledged the CD-i games and never
 will re-license them. Philips no longer makes consoles.
2. **FMV with paid actors.** Like the 3DO and Sega CD, the CD-i leaned on live-action
 video, which layers likeness rights on top of everything else.
3. **No rights holder with an interest.** Philips exited entirely; its games division
 was wound down.

## Appendix: the tails

- **Non-game software (~550 discs).** Encyclopaedias, art and museum collections,
 language courses, Video CD movies. The majority of the catalogue, and deliberately
 out of scope, but the reason the CD-i's "library size" figures vary so wildly
 between sources.
- **Amiga and PC conversions (~60 titles).** Excluded on prior-release grounds.
 Philips leaned on European computer publishers to fill the catalogue.
- **The Nintendo licence (4 titles).** Three Zelda games and one Mario game. The
 most historically consequential block on the platform and the most permanently
 stranded software here; two companies would have to agree, and one no longer
 makes consoles while the other pretends it never happened.

## Coverage gaps

- 9 of ~200 games; the FMV and computer-conversion tails are characterised only.
- Non-game discs excluded by definition.
- No reissue programme exists or is plausible.
