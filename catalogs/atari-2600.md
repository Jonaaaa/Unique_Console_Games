| **Lifespan** | Sep 1977 – 1992; ~30M units sold |
| **Library size** | ~500 licensed titles, plus a large unlicensed and continuing homebrew scene |
| **Backward compat** | The Atari 7800 plays 2600 cartridges. That is BC, **not** another platform. |
| **Last verified** | 2026-07-29 |

## ⚠ The arcade rule inverts here

On the NES, `Donkey Kong` is **excluded** because the Atari 2600 and ColecoVision
already had it in 1982. Go back one generation and the 2600 *is* that earlier
platform, so arcade conversions that are disqualified everywhere else are
legitimate first home releases here.

That makes the 2600 the platform where "arcades are out of scope" does the most
constructive work in the whole repo. `Space Invaders`, `Missile Command`, `Adventure`
and `Combat` are all catalogued as 2600 debuts.

The one caveat: the 2600 was **not** the first home console. The Magnavox Odyssey
(1972) and Fairchild Channel F (1976) preceded it, so a handful of very early titles
are disqualified on prior-home-release grounds.

## Summary

| | Count |
|---|---|
| **Debut games** | **22** |
| of which still `Stranded` | 7 |
| of which since `Ported` | 14 |
| **Contested** | 1 |

**Why the 2600 strands games.** Atari has been diligent, *Atari 50: The
Anniversary Celebration* (2022), the Flashback consoles and *Atari Vault* between them
cover most of what Atari owns. So `Stranded` here isolates two specific groups:

1. **Third-party licences Atari never owned.** `E.T.` needs Universal, `Raiders of
 the Lost Ark` needs Lucasfilm and Paramount, `Space Invaders` needs Taito. Atari
 can reissue its own games and not these.
2. **The unlicensed tail.** Advergames made for cereal and pet-food companies, and
 the notorious adult titles published without Atari's approval, have no legitimate
 route to reissue and never will.

## Contested

| Title | Year | Verdict | Case for including | Case against |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Halo 2600 | 2010 | **Include** (`Stranded`) | An original game written for 2600 hardware and sold as a physical cartridge. The platform's homebrew scene has been commercially active for decades and produces genuine debuts. | It is unlicensed homebrew using someone else's IP, released 18 years after the console was discontinued. If homebrew counts, the 2600's library grows by several hundred titles not covered here. |

## Appendix: the tails

- **Licensed tie-ins (~80 titles).** Film, TV and comic licences from Atari, Parker
 Brothers, Coleco and 20th Century Fox. Almost all stranded, for the ordinary reason.
- **Advergames (~10 titles).** Purina, General Foods, Johnson & Johnson. Mail-order
 only, brand-owned, and the most reliably unreissuable category on the platform.
- **Unlicensed and adult titles (~30).** Published against Atari's wishes; no route
 to legitimate reissue.
- **The 1982–83 shovelware flood (~150 titles).** The volume of low-quality
 third-party product that triggered the 1983 crash. Historically important as a
 phenomenon, individually worthless, and entirely stranded.
- **Homebrew (~300+ titles and counting).** The 2600 has the most active homebrew
 scene of any platform here, with commercially sold cartridges still being produced.
 Out of scope beyond the `Halo 2600` example.

## Coverage gaps

- 20 of ~500 licensed titles. This is the marquee layer plus one representative of
 each stranding cause.
- Homebrew is deliberately excluded except where notable.
- Atari's reissue programme is active; `Ported` may grow for titles Atari owns.
