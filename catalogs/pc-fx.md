## ⚠ The console with no polygons, in 1994

NEC's successor to the PC Engine launched three weeks after the PlayStation with
**no 3D polygon hardware at all**. It was built instead for full-motion video and
sprite work, on the theory that Japan's anime market would carry it.

It did not. The PC-FX sold ~400,000 units in Japan only and its library is roughly
**80% visual novels and dating sims**. Combined with NEC's exit from hardware and
Hudson's absorption into Konami, this is the most completely stranded platform
here: essentially nothing has ever been reissued, and essentially nothing ever
will be.

**Why the PC-FX strands games.** Everything stacks against it at once:

1. **Japan-only, ~400k units.** No Western rights history exists for anything.
2. **Licensed anime dominates.** Most of the library adapts existing series, so each
 title needs a rights holder, a music clearance and often a voice cast re-cleared.
3. **No rights holder with an interest.** NEC left hardware; Hudson was absorbed by
 Konami, which reissues PC Engine titles and has never touched the PC-FX.

**This is the only platform here with a `Ported` count of zero.**

## Appendix: the tail

- **Visual novels and dating sims (~45 titles).** Roughly three-quarters of the
 library. Most carry licensed anime properties, full voice casts and licensed music
, three independent clearance problems each, for a Japan-only audience of 400,000.
 Nothing here will ever be reissued.
- **Anime tie-ins.** `Sailor Moon`, `Zenki`, `Ruin Arm` and similar. Live licences,
 dead platform.
- **The PC-FXGA.** A PC expansion card sold to developers and hobbyists that ran
 PC-FX software. A distribution curiosity, not a separate library.

## Coverage gaps

- 9 of ~62 titles, and the remainder is a genre poorly placed to
 document, Japanese visual novels with no English-language records.
- Zero reissues means these `Stranded` claims are the most stable here.
