## Summary

| | Count |
|---|---|
| **Debut games** | **40** |
| of which still `Stranded` | 17 |
| of which since `Ported` | 23 |
| **Contested** | 1 |

**Why the Dreamcast strands games.** Unusually, the Dreamcast's *canon* escaped
almost completely. Sega exited hardware, became a third-party publisher, and
promptly re-sold its best Dreamcast games onto PS2, then again in HD onto PS3, Xbox
360 and PC. `Shenmue`, `Jet Set Radio`, `Sonic Adventure`, `Crazy Taxi`, `Skies of
Arcadia`, `Space Channel 5` and `Rez` all left.

What remains stranded splits three ways:

1. **Peripheral and hardware dependency.** `Seaman` needs the Dreamcast microphone
 and tracks the real-world calendar. `Samba de Amigo` needs maraca controllers.
 Several games wrote minigames to the **VMU**, a memory card with its own screen
 and buttons, content that exists nowhere else and cannot be reproduced.
2. **Dead servers.** The Dreamcast pioneered console online play, and SegaNet closed
 in 2003. `Phantasy Star Online` survived by moving platforms; games whose online
 mode *was* the game did not.
3. **Kenji Eno and the Japanese tail.** Warp's games and a long list of Japan-only
 releases have no Western rights history and no surviving publisher.

## Contested

| Title | Year | Verdict | Case for including | Case against |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Samba de Amigo | 1999 | **Include** (`Stranded`) | The Dreamcast game is the first home version, and neither the 2008 Wii game nor 2023's *Party Central* is a port; both have different tracklists and were built separately. | Sega markets all three as the same series, so a reasonable reading treats the Wii release as the Dreamcast game reaching another platform, which would make it `Ported`. |

## Appendix: the tails

- **Japan-only releases (~250 titles).** Visual novels, dating sims, and the deep
 post-discontinuation shmup catalogue from G.rev, Milestone and Triangle Service.
 Several shipped for a console its manufacturer had already abandoned.
- **VMU minigames.** The Visual Memory Unit was a memory card with a screen, a
 d-pad and buttons. Games wrote downloadable minigames to it, `Sonic Adventure`'s
 Chao raising, `Skies of Arcadia`'s pinta quest. **This content exists nowhere
 else**, has never been reissued in any form, and requires both a working VMU and
 its battery. Nothing about it has ever been reissued.
- **Online modes.** SegaNet and Dreamarena closed in 2003. Fan-run replacement
 servers exist for some titles, but they are unofficial and do not change status.
- **Indie homebrew (2002–present).** A small commercial scene still presses
 Dreamcast discs. Unlicensed and out of scope, but worth knowing it exists.

## Coverage gaps

- The Japanese library is only spot-covered.
- VMU content is characterised, not enumerated.
- Sports annuals (2K series, NFL 2K) omitted as low-signal; almost all stranded on
 league licences.
