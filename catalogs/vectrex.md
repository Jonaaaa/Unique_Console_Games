## ⚠ The only vector-display console ever sold

The Vectrex has a stranding cause nothing else shares: a **built-in vector monitor**. It draws lines directly with an electron beam rather than
filling a pixel grid, so its games are crisp glowing wireframes at any scale, and a
raster screen cannot reproduce that, only approximate it.

It also shipped with **plastic screen overlays** for colour, since the display is
monochrome, and had the first commercial **3D headset** as an add-on in 1983.

Milton Bradley acquired GCE and killed the platform during the 1983 crash. The rights
have since passed through Hasbro, which **released them for non-commercial
use**, seeding a homebrew scene that still produces cartridges.

**Why the Vectrex strands games.** The display, primarily: any re-release on modern
hardware is a simulation of a vector monitor, not the thing itself, and the overlays
are physical. Beyond that, Hasbro has no commercial interest, and several titles were
arcade licences (`Berzerk`, `Scramble`, `Star Trek`) whose rights sit elsewhere.

Another platform with a `Ported` count of **zero**.
