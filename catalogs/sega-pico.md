## ⚠ A children's educational console

The Pico was a **cartridge-based console for young children**: it connected to a TV,
took "Storyware" cartridges that doubled as physical picture books, and used a stylus
and a pressure-sensitive drawing pad rather than a controller. Sega sold 3.4 million
of them, mostly in Japan, and supported the line for twelve years.

It is included because it is a TV-connected, cartridge-based Sega console with a
software library, but it is an edutainment device, and the "games" are activity
collections built around licensed children's characters. That combination makes it, in
practice, a platform with no reissues of any kind.

**Why the Pico strands games.** Three walls, each sufficient on its own:

1. **Every title is a licensed children's property**: Sonic, Disney, Hello Kitty,
 Anpanman, Doraemon, Thomas the Tank Engine. These are live licences whose owners
 have no interest in a 1990s edutainment reissue.
2. **The hardware is the interface.** Software expects a stylus, a drawing tablet and
 a physical book whose pages the console detects. Reproducing that is closer to
 `Wonderbook` than to a normal port.
3. **Sega has never acknowledged the line.** No Pico title appears in any Sega
 collection, Mini console or Virtual Console service.

**This is the third platform here with a `Ported` count of zero**, alongside the
[PC-FX](pc-fx.md) and [N-Gage](n-gage.md).

## Appendix: the tail

- **Licensed children's properties (~280 titles).** Sonic, Disney, Sanrio, Anpanman,
 Doraemon, Thomas the Tank Engine, Pokémon, Crayon Shin-chan, Ultraman. Effectively
 the whole library. Every one carries a live licence.
- **Japan-only releases (~230).** The Pico sold in Japan, with limited Western
 releases in the mid-1990s and a longer run in China.
- **Advanced Pico Beena (~100 titles, 2005–13).** Sega's successor, Japan-only,
 supported until 2013, which makes it one of Sega's **longest-supported hardware
 lines**, and one nobody outside Japan knows exists. Equally stranded.

## Coverage gaps

- **The largest proportional gap here**: 7 representative entries out of ~400
 across both systems. English-language documentation is close to nonexistent.
- The Beena is characterised in the appendix rather than given its own file.
- Zero reissues, so these `Stranded` claims are maximally stable.
