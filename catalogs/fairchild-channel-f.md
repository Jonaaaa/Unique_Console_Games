## ⚠ The first programmable cartridge console

The Channel F beat the Atari 2600 to market by a year and was **the first console with
a microprocessor and interchangeable ROM cartridges**, the model every console since
has followed. It also introduced the **pause button** and, in `Video Whizball`, the
first in-game credited developer name.

Fairchild sold the business to Zircon in 1979, and Zircon is long gone. Nothing has
ever been reissued, which makes this the earliest platform here whose stranding
cause is the ordinary one: no rights holder with an interest.

**Why the Channel F strands games.** Fairchild exited consumer electronics, Zircon
absorbed and then abandoned the platform, and the library is 27 cartridges for a
console that sold a quarter of a million units in the 1970s. There is no rights holder,
no commercial constituency and no reissue programme, one of several platforms here
with a `Ported` count of **zero**.
