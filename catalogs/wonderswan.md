## ⚠ A wholly Japanese platform

The WonderSwan was designed by **Gunpei Yokoi** — creator of the Game Boy — after he
left Nintendo, and it was his last project before his death in 1997. It sold 3.5
million units on the strength of a Square partnership at a moment when Square and
Nintendo had fallen out, which is why the early *Final Fantasy* remakes appeared here
rather than on Game Boy Color.

**Nothing was ever released outside Japan**, so unlike every other platform in this
repo there is no Western rights history to build a reissue on. Bandai merged into
Bandai Namco in 2005 and has never revisited the hardware.

**Why the WonderSwan strands games.** Three causes stack, and together they make
this the most reliably stranded platform here after the Saturn's Japanese tail.

1. **No Western release, ever.** No localisation exists to reuse, and no Western
 publisher holds any rights.
2. **Licensed anime tie-ins dominate.** Digimon, Gundam, One Piece and Guilty Gear
 licences on a dead Japanese handheld are not economically re-clearable.
3. **Bandai has no reissue programme.** Bandai Namco reissues its arcade and console
 catalogue but has never touched WonderSwan.

The exceptions are instructive: the three `Ported` entries all escaped because a
**different company** owned them and revived them for its own reasons.

## Appendix — the tail

- **Licensed anime tie-ins (~90 titles).** Digimon, Gundam, One Piece, Dragon Ball,
 Crayon Shin-chan, Hunter × Hunter. Bandai was a toy company first, and its handheld
 library reflects it. Every one of these carries a live licence that is worth more
 applied to new products than to reissues.
- **Square remakes (~8 titles).** Historically significant as the origin of Square's
 remake strategy, but excluded here as remakes. All were superseded by GBA, PSP and
 mobile versions.
- **Mahjong, pachinko and quiz titles (~40).** The standard Japanese handheld tail.

## Coverage gaps

- **The largest proportional gap here.** ~15 of ~200 titles are catalogued,
 and English-language documentation of the library is thin.
- No reissue programme exists, so `Ported` is very unlikely to grow — which makes
 these `Stranded` claims among the most stable here.
