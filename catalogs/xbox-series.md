## ⚠ This is the thinnest file in the repo, and it should be

Three of Microsoft's own policies each independently disqualify most of this
console's library:

1. **Cross-gen sim-shipping.** Almost every Microsoft release from 2020–2023 shipped
   on Xbox One the same day. Those are `Sim-ship` entries belonging to **both** files,
   or Xbox One debuts outright.
2. **Day-one PC.** Every first-party title launches on Windows simultaneously. Under
   the PC rule that keeps them `Stranded` rather than `Ported` — but it also means
   there is nothing distinctive about the console SKU.
3. **The 2024 multiplatform pivot.** Microsoft now ships to PS5 and Switch. Several
   Series-era titles have already moved, so `Ported` is growing while the console is
   still current — the opposite of every retired platform here.

Net effect: a very small number of games genuinely debuted on Series X|S **and only
there**. Padding this file would mean breaking the ruleset.

**Why the Series X|S strands games.** Almost nothing here is stranded by an
*obstacle*. There are no dead peripherals, no lost source code, no expired anime
licences. What remains stranded is stranded because **Microsoft has not yet chosen
to move it** — and the company has demonstrated since 2024 that it will. This is
the least permanent `Stranded` column in the repo by a wide margin.

The one durable cause is the familiar one: **licensed cars**. `Forza Motorsport`
(2023) will eventually follow every previous Forza into delisting.

## Appendix — why there is no tail

The Series X|S has no shovelware tail, no Japan-only tail and no peripheral tail —
the three things that fill every other appendix in this repo. Its library is
overwhelmingly:

- **Cross-gen releases** belonging to Xbox One,
- **Multiplatform third-party games** that never qualify, and
- **Backward-compatible software** from three earlier Xbox generations.

For a collector this is the least interesting console in the roster, and that is a
real finding rather than a gap: **Microsoft has spent this generation deliberately
making its software available everywhere**, which is the opposite of what creates
stranded games. Game Pass, day-one PC, four generations of BC and now PlayStation
ports all work against exclusivity.
