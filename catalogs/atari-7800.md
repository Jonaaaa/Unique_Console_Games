## ⚠ A console released two years late

The 7800 was finished in **1984** and shelved when Warner sold Atari's consumer
division mid-launch. It reached shelves in 1986, into a market the NES already owned.
Its library reflects that: mostly arcade conversions designed in 1984, plus a small
run of genuinely original late titles from 1988–90 when Atari briefly reinvested.

Those late originals are where the interesting entries are — and most of them are
stranded, because Atari's reissue programme concentrates on the 2600 and the arcade
catalogue.

**Why the 7800 strands games.** Its arcade conversions mostly fail the debut test —
`Ms. Pac-Man`, `Galaga`, `Joust`, `Centipede` all had earlier home versions. What
qualifies is the small set of 7800-original games, and those are stranded because the
platform sold under 4 million units into a market dominated by Nintendo, and because
several of the licences involved were never Atari's.

*Atari 50* (2022) rescued a handful — notably `Ninja Golf`, the platform's cult
favourite — which shows the barrier is commercial interest rather than rights.

## Appendix — the tail

- **Arcade conversions (~30 titles).** The 1984-designed core of the library. Nearly
  all fail the debut test because 2600 or 5200 versions came first.
- **Home computer conversions (~12 titles).** Excluded on the same principle as
  PC-first games.
- **XG-1 light-gun titles (3).** `Alien Brigade`, `Meltdown` and `Crossbow` need a
  light gun that only works on a CRT — the platform's most permanently locked block.
- **Unreleased prototypes.** Several finished 7800 games from 1984 were never
  published when the launch was shelved; homebrew publishers have since released some.
  Out of scope.

## Coverage gaps

- Complete for identifiable debuts; the 1984-designed unreleased prototypes are not
  catalogued.
- Homebrew and later reproduction cartridges are out of scope.
